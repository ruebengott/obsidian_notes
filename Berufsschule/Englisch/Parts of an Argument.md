# Parts of an Argument

> [!summary] Kurz & knapp
> Ein vollständiges Argument (nach dem Toulmin-Modell) besteht aus **Claim** (Behauptung), **Evidence** (Beleg), **Warrant** (Verbindung Beleg ↔ Behauptung), **Backing** (zusätzliche Stütze), **Rebuttal** (Gegenargument widerlegen) und **Concession & Qualification** (Gegenargument teilweise anerkennen, dann zurück zum Thema).

## Die Bausteine
| Part | Explanation | Example (Wetter) |
|---|---|---|
| **Claim** | topic + assertion – die Behauptung | *It is going to rain later today.* |
| **Evidence** | supports your claim; the reason to accept the assertion | *Just look at those huge black clouds on the eastern horizon.* |
| **Warrant** | explains **how or why** the evidence is linked to the claim | *Dark clouds hold lots of water, which falls to earth.* |
| **Backing** | MORE: secondary evidence, expanded warrants, details – *„help critical thinkers to think more“* | *Up here on the coast, dark clouds always mean heavy rains. While most smaller weather fronts come from the west, our really heavy storms come in from the east.* |
| **Rebuttal** | anticipate counterarguments (counter-claims), say what is **wrong** about them – but be tactful | *The weather forecaster on national TV said there's only a slight chance of rain, but he is based in Berlin. We up here in the north know about these kinds of dark eastern coastal clouds.* |
| **Concession & Qualification** | anticipate counterarguments, admit what is **right** about them, then **get back on your topic** | *Of course, it could blow over the city and not rain in Flensburg, but it will certainly pour somewhere nearby. If winds stay light, the rain might be delayed until after supper, but we are bound to get soaked eventually.* |

## Nützliche Redemittel
| Part | Phrases |
|---|---|
| Claim | *I believe that … / In my opinion, … / It is clear that …* |
| Evidence | *For example, … / According to … / Studies show that …* |
| Warrant | *This shows that … / This means that … / Because …* |
| Backing | *Furthermore, … / In addition, … / Experts also point out that …* |
| Rebuttal | *Some people argue that …, but … / However, this ignores …* |
| Concession | *Admittedly, … / Of course, … / It is true that …, but …* |

> [!tip] Merke
> **Rebuttal** = sagen, was am Gegenargument **falsch** ist. **Concession** = zugeben, was daran **richtig** ist – und dann zurück zur eigenen Position.

## Lernfragen
> [!question]- Was ist ein *warrant*?
> Die Erklärung, **warum** der Beleg (evidence) die Behauptung (claim) stützt.

> [!question]- Unterschied zwischen Rebuttal und Concession?
> Rebuttal widerlegt ein Gegenargument; Concession räumt ein, was am Gegenargument stimmt, und kehrt dann zur eigenen These zurück.

> [!question]- Woraus besteht ein Claim?
> Aus *topic + assertion* (Thema + Behauptung).
